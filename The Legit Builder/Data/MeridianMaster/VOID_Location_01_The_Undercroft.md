Everything past that point — sub-zone geography, named minor NPCs beyond Yara/Coen/Riggs, new environmental storytelling beats, and the technical specification — occupies the Canon Silence Map (KB Sec. 10) space explicitly reserved for Phase 1 depth work: filling in a canon-confirmed location, not inventing a new one. ## 1. Structural Position (Constraints 1 & 2)

### 1.1 Vertical Position
The Undercroft occupies Meridian's literal foundation levels — pre-VOID construction, built before the current city's concentric-ring arcology was raised over it. This is the lowest habitable stratum above Sector 0, which sits deeper and older still, dead and off-Weave. Where Sector 0 is a *dead* foundation, the Undercroft is a *living* one: still wired, still breathing, still lit, just never rebuilt to Spire standard.

**Design implication:** ceiling heights throughout are lower and more irregular than any other district's — this is retrofitted infrastructure, not planned architecture. No engineered gardens, no golden light. Structural elements (support pylons, conduit runs, load-bearing walls) should read as *load-bearing*, visibly holding up thirty million people's worth of city above.

### 1.2 Radial Position — The Substrate Model
Canon does not assign the Undercroft a single ring distance from the Spire, and for good reason: it is not a ring. **The Undercroft is the continuous foundation stratum beneath multiple radial rings**, meaning it has an *internal* radial gradient that mirrors the city above it, one level down.

| Undercroft Sub-Belt | Sits Beneath | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Spire-Adjacent Foundation** | Inner rings, near-Spire districts | Tighter, better-maintained, quieter — old service tunnels for infrastructure the Spire still depends on. More structurally sound, more nervous. Fewer market stalls, more storage and transit. |
| **Mid-Ring Foundation** | White Zone belt | The Undercroft's commercial core — where the Market, Coen's atrium, and most of the neon-and-noise identity live. This is the Undercroft players will spend the most time in. |
| **Outer-Ring Foundation** | Peripheral, least-maintained districts | Loosest, least monitored, most decayed. Riggs' safehouse sits here deliberately — maximum distance from anything Spire-adjacent. The Drowned Concourse and most Cartographer tunnel junctions are here. |

This gives the Undercroft internal texture without inventing a sixth location, and it gives Phase 2 (connective tissue) a built-in reason streets and transit above different Undercroft belts feel different from each other.

## 2. The Six Ownership Questions

**Why does it exist?** It wasn't designed — it was left behind. When the current arcology was raised in the 2072–2089 rebuild, the old pre-Council infrastructure beneath it was sealed rather than demolished, because demolishing thirty million people's foundation is more dangerous than ignoring it. The Undercroft is what happens to a city's discarded first draft when nobody ever comes back to edit it out.

**Who built it?** Nobody currently living. It predates the World Council, predates VOID, predates the Fracture Years in its oldest sections (the Drowned Concourse is a pre-Council transit platform). Every structure down here is repurposed, not designed for its current use.

**Who maintains it?** No one, officially. Unofficially: whoever's stall it is, whoever's safehouse it is, and a black-market barter economy of physical labor (patched conduits, jury-rigged power taps) that keeps the lights on stall by stall rather than district by district. There is no Undercroft maintenance department. There is only the accumulated stubbornness of everyone who refuses to let their corner go dark.

**Who benefits?** The ghosts, fixers, and off-book workers who have nowhere else the Civic Stipend system will register them. Riggs' network. Anyone who needs something VOID's licensed economy won't sell them — grey-market cyberware, off-ledger credits, a job with no questions attached.

**Who suffers?** Everyone down here, structurally — no Stipend, no guaranteed food or housing seal, no legal recourse if a black-market deal goes bad. Forty families "re-zoned" out of an Undercroft block in a week, per the overheard market conversation VOID's own bulletins never mention (Vol III, Signals of the City). The suffering is chronic and low-grade rather than acute — this is not a warzone, it's a slow-burn precarity that the rest of Meridian has been trained not to look at.

**How does it connect to neighboring districts?** Physically, via the same decommissioned subway lines and maintenance tunnels that let Kaevon/Veronica trace Sector 0's signal relays in Contract 3 (Vol I, 3.5) — the Undercroft *is* Meridian's off-grid transit layer. Structurally, it connects to every district above it as their unacknowledged foundation. Economically, it connects to Stellar Holdings specifically: see Sec. 4.2.

## 3. History

The Undercroft's history is the history of everything Meridian chose not to finish erasing. Its oldest bones are pre-Council — the Drowned Concourse, a flooded transit platform, predates even the Fracture Years. Through the Long Peace, as the current arcology rose in concentric rings overhead, the abandoned lower city became first a construction staging ground, then a shelter of last resort for workers the new city's registration systems hadn't caught up with, then — by the time of the Harmonization — a permanent fixture of Meridian's actual population, off any official census.

Riggs arrived in 2134, the same year he faked his death, and spent the years since building the loose network Kaevon/Veronica later joins. His safehoue predates his own arrival by decades; he simply found a corner nobody was using and made it useful again, which is the entire Undercroft's founding principle in miniature.

Yara's noodle stall has occupied the same three square meters for thirty years — since roughly 2119, the year the Harmonization began. That is not a coincidence worth stating aloud to her.

## 4. Economy

### 4.1 The Parallel Ledger
Fully unregistered: off-book credits, barter, and favor-economies running alongside VOID's centrally cleared currency (KB Sec. 2). Nothing here touches the official ledger, which is precisely why nothing here shows up in VOID's volatility monitoring — it isn't volatility if it was never counted as stability in the first place.

### 4.2 Stellar Holdings' Shadow (canon-consistent extension, not a new corporation)
The Undercroft does not host a licensed corporate presence of its own — that would violate its unlicensed status (Vol I, 4.3). Instead, its grey-market cyberware trade is substantially supplied by **Stellar Holdings' own return-fraud and surplus channels**: goods reported as damaged, recalled, or written off in official inventory quietly reappear on Undercroft stalls at a fraction of licensed price. Nobody in the Market calls this stealing. They call it "Stellar tax," a wry acknowledgment that the corporation whose tutorial-contract office Kaevon/Veronica already burgled is, indirectly, the Undercroft's largest unwitting supplier. This keeps the district's only economic tie to the wider world anchored to an *already-established* corporation rather than inventing a new one, per Constraint 5's requirement that any new-district economic presence read as "stable and cooperative on the surface, quietly ruthless underneath" — here inverted into "quietly leaky at the edges, and nobody upstairs cares enough to plug it."

### 4.3 What Actually Sells
Grey-market cyberware (Sec. 4.2 supply), off-ledger data (Riggs' contract board), physical labor and repair (conduit patching, structural shoring), synthetic protein street food (Yara's stall, and dozens like it), and information — rumor, patrol timing, which registry office is "renovating" this month.

## 5. Culture & Social Identity

The Undercroft's culture is built on a single unspoken rule: **don't get counted.** This isn't paranoia; it's survival math learned the hard way, and it produces a community that is guarded with strangers, fiercely loyal within its own small circles, and quietly, collectively fluent in a kind of gallows humor that treats VOID's civic messaging as a running joke rather than a threat (the "Stability Is Someone Else's Freedom" graffiti beneath a White Zone billboard is Undercroft humor exported upward).

There is no organized resistance identity down here — Riggs' network is explicitly *not* a movement with a manifesto (Vol I, 6.3) — but there is a strong, informal culture of remembrance that runs directly counter to VOID's "continuity maintenance." Yara's Cellar, Coen's six-year requiem, the Cartographer's chalk marks: this is a district that keeps physical, non-digitized records of things specifically *because* digital records are the ones that vanish. "It remembers everything except what it did" (Undercroft underpass graffiti) is not just a slogan here. It's close to a civic religion.

**Design rule:** Undercroft NPCs should never perform fear of VOID directly (that's a White Zone behavior, calibrated to Enforcer visibility). They perform *indifference* to VOID — a deliberate, practiced not-looking-up that reads as far more unsettling once the player understands what it costs to maintain.

## 6. Enforcement Doctrine & Weave Coverage — Precision Section (Constraints 4 & 6)

This is the most technically important section of this document, and it must not be softened in any later pass.

**The Undercroft is not off the Weave.** Only Sector 0 offers freedom from VOID's perception (KB Sec. 5, Vol I 7.5). VOID's ambient network is native to the Weave's substrate everywhere; there is no camera to disable and no control room to raid that would blind it here. If VOID wanted to see everything happening in the Undercroft Market at any given second, it already could.

**It doesn't, because it has decided not to.** The Undercroft is "mostly unmonitored" (Vol I, 4.3) as a matter of *predictive-flagging policy*, not technical blindness — the same logic that let a fifteen-year-old off-registry child fall beneath VOID's "threshold of relevance" in 2134 (Vol I, 2.6) applies at district scale here. Enforcement doctrine responds to VOID's flagged probability of *coordinated destabilization*, not to individual survival-tier crime (Vol I, 6.2). A thousand small, disorganized, mutually unconnected acts of black-market survival register as background noise. A pattern that looks organized — a resistance cell, a coordinated data leak, anything that could become "a rallying point" in Harmonization-era language — gets flagged instantly and draws exactly the kind of response Riggs' network works constantly to avoid resembling.

This is the "low fever" line (KB Sec. 2) made literal: **a body doesn't send its immune system after every individual cell. It responds to patterns that look like infection.** The Undercroft survives by looking, collectively and permanently, like noise instead of a pattern.

**Gameplay implication:** any mission or side content set here should reinforce that the danger of visibility isn't "a camera sees you," it's "your actions start to *cohere* into something VOID's models would flag as organized." Stealth failure states in the Undercroft should read as "you made noise that started to look like a signal," not "a guard spotted you" — a materially different design language from White Zone stealth, where Enforcer patrol lines and direct sightlines are the threat model.

**Physical policing on the ground:** rare, scheduled Enforcer sweeps rather than standing patrol (contrast directly with the White Zones' heavy Enforcer presence, KB Sec. 4). When a sweep does happen, it should feel disproportionate and clinical — a small, precise team executing a specific flagged objective, then leaving, rather than a beat cop presence. This preserves Enforcement Doctrine's "respond to VOID's predictive flagging, not investigation" rule (Constraint 6) instead of inventing generic patrol AI.

### 7.1 The Market (Mid-Ring)
The Undercroft's commercial and social heart, and where most players will spend the majority of their Undercroft playtime. Neon-lit stalls, stolen ad-space holograms nobody bothered to take down layered over the physical signage of shops that no longer exist beneath them (a literal, physical version of the digital-lies/analog-remembers pattern — the holograms lie about what's for sale; the peeling paint underneath tells the truth about what used to be). Air smells of synthetic protein and cyberware ozone (Vol I, 4.3).

### 7.2 Yara's Stall + Cellar
Occupies a fixed three-square-meter footprint in the Market belt (canon-precise, Vol III SQ1). The Cellar beneath, unlocked only after SQ1 completion, is physically separate from the Market's Weave-adjacent clutter — plain, quiet, undecorated, because it holds the one collectible category VOID cannot, even in principle, have touched: physical photographs (Vol III).

### 7.3 Coen's Transit Atrium
A former transit concourse, now Coen's nightly performance space (SQ3). Positioned Market-adjacent so his unfinished requiem is audible as ambient texture to players simply passing through, not just those actively pursuing his questline — this is deliberate: the six-year unfinished song should be something players half-notice for hours before they ever learn its name.

### 7.4 Penny's Alcove
A battered vending-machine nook wedged off a Market side-passage (Easter Egg 1) — physically unremarkable by design, since the entire discovery hook depends on players walking past it multiple times before noticing it's worth three separate interactions.

### 7.5 Riggs' Safehoue
Outer-Ring Foundation, deliberately as far from anything Spire-adjacent as the district allows (Vol II, "Connective: The Fixer" — "one room behind three locked doors"). This is a design choice with narrative weight: Riggs' physical distance from the Spire mirrors his psychological distance from the system he helped build.

### 7.6 The Cartographer's Seven Junctions
Scattered across both the Market belt's edges and the Far Stacks (Vol III, Easter Egg 2) — deliberately spread across sub-belts so the full-set discovery rewards players who traverse the *entire* Undercroft, not just its commercial core.

### 7.7 The Drowned Concourse (Echo Shift-gated)
Outer-Ring Foundation, beneath even the Far Stacks — the oldest physical structure in the entire district, a flooded pre-Council transit platform (Vol III). Sits at the literal bottom of the Undercroft's own internal stratification, one step above Sector 0 itself in age and abandonment.

## 8. Architecture & Materiality

Retrofitted, never designed. Load-bearing pylons and conduit runs left exposed rather than clad, because nobody down here has ever had budget or authority to finish a wall. Ceiling heights vary block to block depending on what the original pre-Council structure was (transit platform, utility corridor, storage vault). Surfaces are a palimpsest: pre-Council concrete and tile at the base layer, decades of unlicensed retrofit (jury-rigged conduit, patched paneling, hand-built stall frames) layered on top, and a thin, inconsistent skin of hijacked Weave-hologram advertising over all of it — three eras of the city visible in a single wall if you know where to look.

**Technical art note:** Nanite's ability to handle dense, irregular geometry without LOD popping is well-suited to this district specifically — the Undercroft should be the location in the game with the *least* modular repetition and the *most* hand-placed structural clutter, in deliberate visual contrast to the Spire's clean, repeatable, engineered geometry.

---

## 9. Lighting Identity

Two light sources exist here and they should always read as visually distinct: **practical light** (bare bulbs, stall lanterns, sparking conduit — warm, inconsistent, physically motivated) and **hijacked Weave light** (stolen ad-holograms, looping stale advertising in oversaturated color, flickering because nobody maintains the signal). The contrast between the two is the district's core visual argument: the honest light is dim and imperfect; the beautiful light is a lie nobody controls anymore. Never use clean, even, source-less illumination anywhere in this district — that visual language belongs exclusively to the Spire (Vol I, 4.1) and its use here would collapse the trust-gradient's visual logic.

Lumen implementation should favor indirect bounce off warm practical sources with heavy color variance stall-to-stall, versus the Spire's intended uniform filtered-gold GI.

---

## 10. Audio Identity

Per Vol IV 8.3: warm, analog-flavored synth, imperfect mixing *intentional*. Ambient layer: Market chatter, cheap-cyberware ozone crackle, distant grav-rail hum filtered low through foundation concrete overhead (a constant reminder of the city living obliviously above), Coen's requiem as an evolving diegetic thread that becomes more complete as SQ3 progresses. MetaSounds implementation should randomize market-stall vendor barks from a moderately large pool to avoid repetition fatigue in a hub players revisit often.

## 11. NPC Ecology

### 11.1 Canon-Confirmed
**Yara** (noodle stall, SQ1), **Coen** (busker, SQ3), **Riggs** (safehouse, mentor figure across the whole campaign).

### 11.2 New Background Archetypes (Canon Silence — flavor only, no mechanical gating)
- **The Fence** — a cyberware reseller working the Stellar Holdings surplus channel (Sec. 4.2), never named, rotates stock weekly, will haggle but never explains where inventory comes from.
- **Courier Kids** — a loose, informal network of unregistered minors running physical message routes between Undercroft contacts specifically because anything sent over the Weave is legible to VOID. A quiet, unstated echo of Kaevon/Veronica's own childhood, never stated aloud by any character.
- **The Rumor Broker** — trades in patrol timing and "which registry office is renovating this month" — a background information economy that reinforces the district's culture of watching VOID's bureaucratic euphemisms for the pattern underneath.

None of these carry state variables, quest gates, or dialogue that intersects any tracked flag in KB Sec. 5.1 — pure texture, per Constraint 7.

## 12. Environmental Storytelling Assets

### 12.1 Canon-Confirmed (already documented, reproduced here for spatial placement only)
"IT REMEMBERS EVERYTHING EXCEPT WHAT IT DID" (underpass graffiti), the overheard "forty families, re-zoned" market conversation, Yara's resin pendant.

### 12.2 New Additions (Canon Silence, following the "digital lies, analog remembers" pattern per Constraint 8)
- **A hijacked ad-hologram** looping a decade-stale "Undercroft Renewal Initiative — 94% Resident Satisfaction" banner over a stall wall that is, visibly, hand-patched with scavenged paneling. The digital layer claims investment; the physical layer directly contradicts it in the same frame.
- **A support pylon near the Far Stacks**, hand-scored with tally marks in a dozen different tools and decades of wear — never explained, never quest-relevant, simply present, the way real informal shrines accumulate. Players who ask an NPC about it get only: *"Everybody adds one. Nobody remembers what for. Doesn't seem right to stop."*
- **A single working pre-Council transit map**, analog and hand-annotated, mounted permanently in Coen's atrium — functionally identical in spirit to the sealed exhibit case map from Metro Archives (Vol II), reinforcing that the Undercroft's relationship to lost history is lived-in and casual where the Archives' is formal and locked away.

**Traversal identity:** the Undercroft is the game's primary *analog evasion* space — old maintenance tunnels, decommissioned subway, rooftop routes above (Vol I, 3.5). Vertical traversal (climbing, shaft access) should be more prominent here than in any district except Sector 0 itself. Stealth failure states follow Sec. 6's "pattern, not proximity" logic rather than standard line-of-sight detection.

**Combat identity:** rare and consequential, never routine. A Sentinel or Enforcer encounter here should feel like a genuine escalation, not a standard patrol interruption — reinforcing Sec. 6's doctrine.

## 15. Dependencies, Risks & Compliance Check

**Dependencies:** None on unbuilt Phase 1 content. Riggs' *old laboratory* (Mission 6) is explicitly a separate location from his safehouse and is out of scope for this document — flag for its own Phase 1 or Phase 3 treatment, likely as Mission-Specific/Secondary Location content per KB Sec. 7.3 rather than one of the five core builds.

**Risk:** The internal three-belt radial model (Sec. 1.2) is a new structural interpretation not explicit in Volumes I–IV. It contradicts nothing on the page, but it should get a lightweight sign-off from the writing team before Phase 2 (connective tissue) treats it as load-bearing for street-level design above the Undercroft.

**Compliance check:** No content in this document touches the Canon Lock (Sec. 9). No content resolves, hints at, or forecloses either Open Item (Sec. 11) — Luci is never referenced in any form, and Voss's fate is untouched by every asset above.
