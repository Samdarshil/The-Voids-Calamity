## World Design & Production Document — Location 02: THE WHITE ZONES
### Phase 1, Build Target 2 of 5 | Canon Source: Vol I Ch. 4.2, Vol II Mission 5, Vol III SQ4, Vol IV 3/4/8 | Status: Design Complete, Pending Greybox

## 0. Canon Compliance Statement

Every fact in Sections 1–7 is traceable to Volumes I–IV. Past that point, this document occupies the Canon Silence Map exactly where the Knowledge Base flags it as open: *"additional White Zones (only one is detailed)"* (KB Sec. 10). Nothing below touches the Canon Lock (Sec. 9) or either Open Item (Sec. 11). No content in this package references Luci in any form; no content forecloses any version of Voss's fate.

**A structural note before anything else:** the White Zones are not a single contiguous district the way the Undercroft is. Canon is explicit that they are *distributed civic centers* — plural, standardized, repeated across the mid-tier residential rings (Vol I, 4.2). Designing "the White Zones" therefore means designing two things, not one: **a reusable typology** (the Community Trust Center architectural kit, stamped across the city) and **one fully detailed flagship instance** (the specific Trust Center that hosts Mission 5 and Side Quest 4). This document builds both, and is explicit throughout about which sections apply city-wide and which apply only to the flagship.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The White Zones are VOID's civic hand made visible: clinics, ration distribution, and registration offices, standardized to a single calm architectural language and distributed at regular intervals through Meridian's mid-tier residential rings. They are the most heavily monitored non-Spire spaces in the city — not because VOID suspects the people who use them, but because they are, by design, the point where every registered citizen's continuity data is refreshed most often. Where the Undercroft survives by looking like noise, the White Zones function by asking, gently and constantly, to be looked at. That contrast is the design spine of this entire document.

---

## 2. Structural Position (Constraints 1 & 2)

### 2.1 Radial Position — The Node Network Model
The White Zones sit in **the mid-tier band of residential rings** — far enough from the Spire that no resident mistakes a Trust Center for a seat of power, and far enough from the Undercroft-adjacent outer rings that the psychological break between "civic order" and "edge tolerance" reads clearly the moment a player crosses it. Unlike the Undercroft's continuous substrate (a single structural layer under many rings), the White Zones are **discrete, repeated nodes distributed across that mid-tier band** — the deliberate architectural contrast being: *the Undercroft is one thing everywhere; the White Zones are the same thing everywhere.* Repetition, not continuity, is the network's identity.

### 2.2 Vertical Position
Ground level, low-rise, human-scaled — 2 to 4 stories, always. This is a hard design rule, not a suggestion: Trust Centers must never read as monumental. Their trust-gradient function depends on looking *approachable*, in direct contrast to both the Spire's soaring verticality (power performing effortlessness) and the Undercroft's buried irregularity (survival performing invisibility). A White Zone is architecture performing reassurance, and reassurance, in this world, is always at eye level.

---

## 3. The Six Ownership Questions

**Why does it exist?** Officially, to guarantee every registered citizen's access to clinics, rations, and civic registration close to home. Functionally, it is also VOID's most efficient point of routine population contact — a place citizens *choose* to visit regularly, which makes it a far more elegant continuity-monitoring instrument than surveillance imposed from outside would ever be.

**Who built it?** The World Council's civic infrastructure division, under VOID's own architectural standards program — a genuine, funded, centrally designed bureaucratic entity, unlike the Undercroft's organic accretion. "Community Trust Center" is a branded, standardized typology, the same way a bank or a transit station is instantly recognizable across a real city regardless of neighborhood.

**Who maintains it?** Council civic services, on public budget, to a maintained standard — no jury-rigged conduit here, no stall-by-stall stubbornness. Maintenance is centralized, scheduled, and visible, which is itself part of the messaging: a well-kept building is a trustworthy one.

**Who benefits?** The overwhelming majority of Meridian's thirty million registered residents, materially and genuinely — clean clinics, guaranteed rations, safe civic process. This must not be written as a cynical lie; per Vol I 3.7, daily life for a documented citizen is, by real material measures, extremely good. The horror is not that the benefit is fake. The horror is what the benefit quietly costs.

**Who suffers?** Two distinct groups, and the document should keep them distinct: (1) unregistered ghosts like Kaevon/Veronica, for whom a White Zone is close to the most dangerous physical space in the game outside a mission-critical infiltration; and (2) registered citizens themselves, under a soft-compulsion mechanic — Council civic bulletins state Trust Center visits are *"not mandatory, but... strongly encouraged for optimal Stipend continuity"* (Vol I, 3.7). That single clause is the entire ownership question's answer in miniature: nobody is forced. Everybody complies.

**How does it connect to neighboring districts?** Each Trust Center is deliberately positioned within easy walking distance of the residential population it serves (Vol I, 4.2) — meaning the building's placement *defines* its surrounding neighborhood's civic center in the same way a real town square does. Physically it connects outward via the residential ring's pedestrian grid and inward via grav-rail to the wider transit network. Full residential-ring streetscape design is Phase 2 connective-tissue work (flagged in Sec. 16); this document specifies the White Zone's own footprint and immediate approach only.

## 4. History

No exact founding date for the Community Trust Center program survives in the official record — a detail worth treating as deliberate texture rather than a gap this document needs to paper over. Civic historians (in-fiction) place it broadly in the early-to-mid Long Peace, 2090s–2100s, as VOID's mandate expanded from pure resource logistics into direct civic administration (Vol I, 2.5) and megacity development absorbed the majority of the world's population into managed, VOID-administered structures. The program almost certainly predates the Harmonization by a meaningful margin — Trust Centers are civic infrastructure, not enforcement infrastructure, and their calm branding reads as a Long Peace product, built when the population still needed to be *reassured into* dependence rather than *managed through* one.

That the exact year isn't recorded anywhere citable is itself in-world consistent: unlike the Undercroft's precisely-dated landmarks (Yara's thirty years, Riggs' 2134 arrival), the Trust Center program was never a single event with a ribbon-cutting. It simply became the water the city swims in, one branch opening after another, until nobody alive remembers a residential ring that didn't have one.

## 5. Role in Meridian & Economy of Trust

The White Zones are not commercial space — no stalls, no vendors, no barter economy. Their "economy" is entirely the Civic Stipend system (KB Sec. 2): rations distributed, clinic visits logged, Stipend continuity confirmed. There is no black market equivalent here, no grey zone, because the entire point of the typology is legibility. Where the Undercroft's economy runs on things VOID *doesn't* count, the White Zones' economy runs entirely on things VOID *does*.

---

## 6. Urban Planning & Distribution Logic

**The Kit-of-Parts Principle:** every Trust Center in the network is assembled from the same architectural components (Sec. 8), scaled to local population density but never restyled. This is a deliberate production efficiency as much as a narrative one — a single well-designed typology, re-skinned only by scale and wear, lets engineering populate the entire mid-tier ring network without a bespoke design pass per instance, while reinforcing in-fiction that VOID's civic presence is intentionally, unnervingly uniform.

**Distribution rule:** one Trust Center per residential population catchment, sized to guarantee walkable access for the elderly and mobility-impaired (Vol I, 4.2 implies this via the "close to home" framing). This is the *only* location type in Meridian's five confirmed districts whose placement logic is explicitly population-driven rather than power-driven (Spire) or history-driven (Undercroft, Metro Archives, Sector 0) — worth preserving as a deliberate throughline into Phase 3 new-district work, where any additional Trust Center instances should follow the same population-catchment logic rather than being placed for visual composition alone.

---

## 7. Geography & Vertical Structure

Flat, open, low-rise — the opposite of the Undercroft's cramped irregularity and the Spire's soaring verticality. Generous plaza space fronts every Trust Center, both a genuine accessibility feature and a surveillance one: an open approach means nothing enters or leaves without being seen, by camera or by neighbor. This should be treated as a deliberate design tension throughout every gameplay pass on this location — openness here reads as safety to an NPC and as exposure to the player.

---

## 8. Architecture & Building Typology Kit (City-Wide, Reusable)

| Component | Function | Access Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry Plaza** | Open civic approach, queue management, ambient Weave messaging displays | Fully public |
| **Reception Atrium** | Central hub, calm white-and-soft-blue palette, wayfinding to all wings | Fully public |
| **Clinic Wing** | Basic medical service, health-data logging | Public, logged |
| **Ration Distribution Hall** | Stipend-linked resource collection | Public, logged |
| **Registration Office** | Civic-record updates, identity/continuity confirmation | Public, logged |
| **Administrative Back Offices** | Staff-only civic processing (Talis Bren's domain, canon-confirmed) | Restricted, badge-gated |
| **Records/Audit Closet** | Compressed data storage, secondary server tap | Highly restricted |
| **Enforcer Staging Alcove** | Standing patrol post, rapid-dispatch readiness | Enforcement-only |
| **Rooftop Mechanical Level** | HVAC/utility access, off the public path | Maintenance-only |

This kit is designed to scale: smaller catchments drop the Clinic Wing to a single triage room; larger ones expand the Ration Hall. The Administrative Back Offices, Records Closet, and Enforcer Staging Alcove are present in every instance regardless of scale — they are the network's true structural constant, since they serve VOID's monitoring function rather than the population's.

## 9. The Flagship Instance — Mission 5 / SQ4 Location

This is the one White Zone canon actually details, and the only instance requiring full mission-critical geography.

**Composition:** full kit-of-parts (Sec. 8) at upper-mid scale, serving a dense mid-tier catchment. Mira lives in the residential block the Trust Center anchors — her apartment (Vol II, Mission 5 optional exploration) sits within the immediate walkable catchment, not inside the Trust Center itself. The Mission 5 extraction route therefore runs *from* her apartment block, *through* the neighborhood's pedestrian grid, *into* the Trust Center's plaza and atrium, where Enforcer response converges — giving the escort mission a real spatial arc (residential quiet → open plaza exposure → civic-building gauntlet) rather than a single static room.

**SQ4 relevant geography:** Talis Bren's cover story ("routine visit") gets a player as far as the Reception Atrium and, briefly, the Administrative Back Offices without triggering escalation. The Records/Audit Closet is the boundary where her eight months of quiet work lives, and where VESSEL-9 — assigned specifically to watch VOID's own administrative staff — activates once the audit package is pulled (Vol III, SQ4).

**A new, canon-silence traversal beat:** because Echo Shift is unlocked at the Act 2 hub upgrade, which occurs *before* Mission 4 and therefore before Mission 5 in the mission flow (KB Sec. 6), the ability is available to the player during this mission. This document adds one Echo-Shift-gated secondary route through the Rooftop Mechanical Level as an optional stealth extraction path for Mira — consistent with the ability's established function (temporary removal from VOID's perception to pass monitored barriers) and adding nothing that contradicts the mission's locked outcome (Mira reaches safety regardless of the Nyx branch, per Vol II).

## 10. Road Hierarchy & Pedestrian Networks

**Primary approach road:** fronts the Entry Plaza directly, sized for both pedestrian flow and Enforcer vehicle response — the same road serves reassurance and rapid deployment, which is precisely the point.

**Pedestrian network:** wide, well-lit, unobstructed sightlines from the residential grid to the Trust Center's front doors. No alleys, no blind corners, within the immediate catchment — Undercroft-style irregular traversal has no place here and should not be introduced; it would contradict the district's core visual/gameplay identity.

**Boundary flag:** the wider residential-ring grid beyond the Trust Center's immediate catchment is Phase 2 connective-tissue scope, not detailed here — see Sec. 16.

---

## 11. Metro / Transit Connections

A grav-rail stop sits within short walking distance of every Trust Center instance by design rule (Sec. 6) — Stipend-linked civic errands are meant to be frictionless, reinforcing the "safe transit, guaranteed everything" daily-life messaging (Vol I, 3.7). The flagship instance's stop additionally serves as a rapid Enforcer deployment point during Mission 5's escalation, per Enforcement Doctrine (Sec. 18).

---

## 12. Landmarks

The Entry Plaza's Weave-display column is the closest thing this typology has to a landmark feature — a standardized, branded fixture (identical citywide) cycling civic messaging ("VOID keeps us safe," "Stability is Freedom," Stipend continuity reminders). Its uniformity *is* the landmark: residents orient by it the same way they would by a real town square's monument, except the monument is an advertisement for the system that built it.

---

## 13. Interior Opportunities

Reception Atrium (social stealth, civilian blending), Clinic Wing (queue-based traversal, low tension), Ration Hall (identical), Administrative Back Offices (SQ4's cover-story-gated access tier, moderate tension), Records/Audit Closet (highest interior tension, VESSEL-9), Rooftop Mechanical Level (Echo-Shift-gated, lowest civilian density, highest traversal opportunity).

---

## 14. Environmental Storytelling

### 14.1 Canon-Confirmed
The civic bulletin ("Council confirms Harmonization-era archives unaffected by recent Undercroft network irregularities... remain calm and report unusual signal activity") and the graffiti beneath a White Zone billboard ("STABILITY IS SOMEONE ELSE'S FREEDOM") — both from Vol III's Signals of the City.

### 14.2 New Additions (Canon Silence, "digital lies, analog remembers" pattern)
- **A Stipend-continuity counter** on the Entry Plaza display, ticking upward in real time as citizens check in — a literal, ambient visualization of the "get counted correctly" culture, and a passive tension tool: the player can watch the number climb while deciding whether to risk being added to it.
- **A single scuffed floor tile** in the Reception Atrium, worn pale by decades of the exact same queue path — the one purely analog trace in a building otherwise built entirely of clean surfaces and Weave messaging, following the established pattern in miniature: even a building this controlled still has one thing it didn't design on purpose.
- **A civilian's dropped ration slip**, findable near the Ration Hall, printed rather than digital — a small, mundane detail suggesting the system keeps a physical paper trail specifically for its most vulnerable dependents (the elderly, likely, echoing Mira), quietly humanizing the queue without any dialogue required.

---

## 15. Civilian Life & Culture

Where the Undercroft's unspoken rule is *don't get counted*, the White Zones' is its exact inversion: **get counted correctly, or lose the things counting protects.** This produces a specific, quiet civic anxiety underneath the gratitude culture — residents here should never be written as unhappy, exactly, but as *careful*, in a way that reads as habit rather than fear. NPC barks should model polite, cooperative queue behavior; panic or suspicion should be reserved for moments the player's presence actively destabilizes that calm (Mission 5's escalation).

**Design rule:** civilian NPCs here should never perform overt fear of VOID (that reads as contradicting the district's genuine, real material benefit) but should visibly, subtly prioritize *procedural correctness* — checking a display twice, double-confirming a queue position — a small tell that the soft compulsion (Sec. 3) has become internalized habit rather than active dread.

---

## 16. Security & Enforcement Doctrine

**Direct contrast to the Undercroft (Sec. 6 of that document):** White Zones carry **heavy, standing Enforcer patrol** (Vol I, 4.2) rather than the Undercroft's rare, flagged sweeps. This is not a contradiction of Constraint 6's "predictive flagging, not investigation" doctrine — it's the same doctrine applied to a different baseline risk profile. VOID's models correctly predict that a space this monitored, this legible, and this central to Stipend continuity is exactly where a genuine anomaly (an unregistered ghost, a fugitive, a compromised administrator) is statistically most likely to eventually surface, precisely *because* it's where everyone else complies. Standing patrol here is itself a predictive-flagging output — a permanently elevated baseline threat score for the highest-legibility, highest-value target type in the network, not routine beat policing.

**Flagship-specific escalation (Mission 5):** patrol density increases sharply and visibly once Mira's extraction begins, modeling VOID's real-time flagging response rather than a scripted alarm state — Enforcer units should read as *arriving because the pattern changed*, not because a generic alarm sound played.

---

## 17. Weave Infrastructure

The purest, highest-density expression of VOID's ambient perception network outside the Spire itself. No hijacked ad-space, no signal clutter, no degraded holograms — every display here is centrally curated, current, and calm. This is a deliberate, hard visual/technical contrast to the Undercroft's hacked-and-scavenged Weave presence (that document, Sec. 9): if the Undercroft is the Weave with its polish stripped away, the White Zones are the Weave exactly as VOID intends it to be seen.

---

## 18. Technology

Standard civic-tier tech only: Stipend-linked registration terminals, ration-dispensing systems, basic clinic diagnostic equipment. No ICE constructs beyond standard Sentinel-tier security (Vol I, 6.2) — this typology's actual defense is its Enforcer density and total Weave coverage, not digital countermeasures, which is a meaningful contrast to Stellar Holdings' server-side security in the Tutorial Contract.

---

## 19. Lighting & Color Language

Calm, reassuring white-and-soft-blue palette (Vol I, 4.2) — even, source-diffused, near-shadowless. This is architecture explicitly designed to never let anyone feel like they're somewhere secret. Lighting should read as *slightly* softer and warmer than the Spire's filtered-gold engineered light (that's elite, curated luxury) but categorically cleaner and more even than anything in the Undercroft (that's honest imperfection). White Zone lighting is the visual language of institutional care — and, played correctly against the mission content, institutional care that has quietly become surveillance.

---

## 20. Materials

Smooth, matte-finished panel systems, seamless flooring, minimal visible fixtures or conduit — everything the Undercroft's exposed, load-bearing honesty is not. Wear should be nearly invisible except at the one analog detail specified in Sec. 14.2 (the scuffed queue tile) — a deliberate, singular crack in an otherwise flawless material language.

---

## 21. Ambient Audio

Quiet, low-dynamic-range civic ambience: soft HVAC hum, muted queue chatter, gentle chime tones for registration/ration confirmations. No music proper — the absence of a scored ambient identity (contrast the Undercroft's warm analog synth, Vol IV 8.3) is itself the point: this is a space designed to feel neutral, not to feel like anywhere in particular. MetaSounds implementation should favor near-silence over texture, breaking only for the Mission 5 escalation, where patrol vox and alert tones should intrude sharply against the established quiet — making the calm's absence, once broken, land as a genuine tonal shock.

---

## 22. Gameplay Opportunities

**Combat spaces:** the Records/Audit Closet (VESSEL-9, SQ4) and the Entry Plaza during Mission 5's escalation are the only intended combat-forward spaces in this typology. Everywhere else should actively discourage combat design — a firefight in a Ration Hall should feel like a design violation of the space's identity, not a viable player option the level quietly permits.

**Stealth routes:** social stealth (queue-blending, disguise) is this district's primary player tool, contrasting directly with the Undercroft's physical/analog evasion identity (that document, Sec. 13) and previewing the social-stealth language Mission 7's Spire infiltration will use at a much higher stakes tier — a deliberate mid-game rehearsal for that later mission.

**Traversal:** minimal verticality by design (Sec. 2.2); the Rooftop Mechanical Level (Sec. 9) is the one exception, Echo-Shift-gated, and should be the only place in this typology that rewards a player for leaving the ground floor.

---

## 23. Collectibles & Hidden Areas

Memory Fragments should be sparse here relative to the Undercroft — this is not a district built around remembrance, it's built around forgetting-by-design, so finding one here should feel more dissonant and pointed than finding one in the Undercroft's already-remembrance-soaked culture. The Rooftop Mechanical Level (Echo-Shift-gated, Sec. 9) is the flagship instance's one secret area, holding a single Memory Fragment tied contextually to Mira's story.

## 24. NPC Ecology

### 24.1 Canon-Confirmed
**Talis Bren** (administrator, SQ4), **Mira** (living witness, Mission 5), background civilian queue population, standing Enforcer patrol units, **VESSEL-9** (hidden boss, SQ4).

### 24.2 New Background Archetypes (Canon Silence, flavor only, no state variables)

- **The Regular** — an elderly civilian who visits the same Trust Center at the same time daily, a small, wordless echo of the compulsive-but-not-mandatory Stipend-continuity culture, present as ambient texture only.

## 25. UE5 Technical Notes, World Partition & Streaming Strategy

**World Partition Region:** `WP_WhiteZones_Flagship` for the Mission 5/SQ4 instance. **Recommend a second, lightweight streaming template**, `WP_WhiteZones_NetworkNode_Template`, built from the Sec. 8 kit-of-parts and intended for reuse across any future non-flagship Trust Center placements the connective-tissue or Phase 3 passes require — this is the correct technical expression of the kit-of-parts design principle (Sec. 6) and avoids a bespoke World Partition region per network instance.

**Data Layers (conceptual, content-gating only):**
- `DL_WhiteZone_PublicWings_Base` — always active (Reception, Clinic, Ration Hall, Registration)
- `DL_WhiteZone_AdminBackOffice_SQ4Gated` — active once SQ4 unlocked (post-Mission 5)
- `DL_WhiteZone_RecordsCloset_VESSEL9` — restricted-access, SQ4 critical path only
- `DL_WhiteZone_RooftopMechanical_EchoShiftGated` — requires Echo Shift ability
- `DL_WhiteZone_Mission5_EscalationState` — toggles heavy patrol density during the extraction sequence, reverting after mission completion

**Streaming priority:** Reception Atrium and Entry Plaza should stream at highest priority given constant civilian NPC density; Records Closet and Rooftop Mechanical Level lowest, given restricted/gated access.

## 28. Meridian Integration

The White Zones now establish the **middle** calibration point of the trust gradient, with the Undercroft anchoring the bottom. Structural contrast pairs to carry forward into the Spire document: Weave density (hijacked/clutter → full/curated → presumed maximal), enforcement posture (rare/flagged → heavy/standing → presumed near-total), and architectural verticality (buried/irregular → low-rise/uniform → presumed soaring). Each of these three-point gradients should resolve cleanly once the Spire is built, giving the full trust-gradient axis (KB Sec. 7.1) a complete, internally consistent throughline across all three tiers.
