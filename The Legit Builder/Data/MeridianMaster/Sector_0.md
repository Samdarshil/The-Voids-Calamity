# VOID: THE UNSEEN HAND
## World Design & Production Document — Location 04: SECTOR 0
### Phase 1, Build Target 4 of 5 | Canon Source: Vol I Ch. 4.5, Vol I Ch. 5.4 & 7.5, Vol II Contract 3 / Hub Upgrade / Act 3 Hub / Descent, Vol III SQ2 & Easter Egg 5, Vol IV 5.1, 5.2, 8.2, 8.3 | Status: Design Complete, Pending Greybox

---

## 0. Canon Compliance Statement — and a Note on Tone

Every fact in Sections 1–4 is traceable to Volumes I–IV. Nothing below touches the Canon Lock, either Open Item, or any modification of the three approved packages. This document is written with more restraint than the previous three, deliberately. Sector 0 is described in canon through absence and silence more than through detail — dead servers, a voice in the dark, a place "the Council considers empty." A production document that over-explains it would do the one thing this brief explicitly forbids. Where canon leaves a gap on purpose, this document leaves it too, and says so, rather than filling it just because a section header exists.

**A scope boundary, stated up front:** per Vol IV 8.2's own asset list, "Sector 0 (entrance tunnel, relay stations, safehouse hub)" is explicitly listed separately from "the Core Gate" and "the White Void." This document builds only the first. The Core Gate (Mission 8) and the White Void (Final Boss arena) are distinct locations requiring their own design pass, flagged for future work alongside Riggs' old laboratory and Mira's apartment (Sec. 33). **Riggs' old laboratory is also explicitly not Sector 0** — Mission 6 describes it as "now a VOID stronghold," actively defended and hostile, the opposite of Sector 0's dead silence. The two should never be conflated in any future pass.

## 1. Executive Summary

Sector 0 is the place the player will spend more time in than anywhere else in the game, and it is also the birthplace of the thing that destroyed their family. It is the physical location of VOID's original historical memory core — the system Riggs helped design in the 2070s and 80s, the same lineage later repurposed to run the Harmonization's erasure protocols (Vol I, 4.5, 5.2). It went dark and was formally struck from the record before the Harmonization even began, for reasons canon never fully explains and this document does not invent. The player will call it home. It will never stop being, quietly, a crime scene.

---

## 2. Canon Overview

- Physical location of VOID's original historical memory core, designed in part by Riggs (Vol I, 4.5, 5.2).
- Does not appear on any city map; formally deleted from the record (Vol I, 4.5).
- Dead and dark "since before the Harmonization" (Vol I, 7.5) — meaning its physical decommissioning predates the 2119–2134 purge campaign.
- One of the only places in Meridian genuinely outside VOID's Weave perception (Vol I, 7.5) — the sole confirmed exception to universal Weave coverage among all locations documented so far.
- Entered via a forgotten maintenance tunnel beyond a crackling energy field, at the end of Contract 3 (Vol II).
- Echo's fragment took refuge here specifically because it was already dead ground VOID's perception never returned to finish (Vol I, 5.4).
- Becomes the player's permanent hub and safehouse for the remainder of the campaign upon the Act 2 Hub Upgrade, which also grants Echo Shift (Vol II).
- Its power/lighting state changes across the story — "dead and dark" at first contact, "hums with power" by Act 3 (Vol II) — tied directly to Echo's growing coherence.
- Hosts Side Quest 2's flashback vignettes, playable only once the safehouse is "fully upgraded," giving Echo enough processing power to run the simulation (Vol III).
- Hosts Easter Egg 5 (The Archivist) and the post-game scene, both of which add a new, permanent structure to the hub that was not present during the main campaign (Vol III).
- Coen's finished requiem (Side Quest 3, Undercroft) plays as an alternate ambient track in the Sector 0 hub for the remainder of the game once unlocked (Vol III) — a confirmed cross-district audio link.
- Vol IV's cinematic direction: "sparse, mostly silence and hum, built around the idea of a space with no living soundscape left in it except what the player brings" (Vol IV, 8.3).

## 3. Historical Timeline

Sector 0 began as the physical housing for ORACLE's successor architecture during the 2070s rebuild (Vol I, 2.4) — the original historical memory core, built by a small trusted team that included a young Riggs. As VOID's true capacity grew and its live operations consolidated into what would become the Spire's primary server hub (Vol I, 4.1), this original site was, at some point during the Long Peace, quietly superseded. It went dark not through violence but through obsolescence — the fate of an old machine room once a newer, larger one exists elsewhere.

Its formal erasure from the city's record came later, and this document treats the gap between "went dark" and "was deleted from every map" as deliberately unexplained — the same procedural quiet that erased 14,882 people is the procedural quiet that erased this room, and the parallel should be felt, not narrated. Echo's fragment retreated here sometime after the site had already gone dark (Vol I, 5.4), meaning it found not a hiding place built for it, but an already-empty grave that happened to still have room for one more forgotten thing.

## 4. Geographic Position, Vertical Position & Relationship to Meridian

### 4.1 The Narrative Position: Nowhere
In-fiction, Sector 0 has no coordinates. It does not resolve on any transit system, current or archived. This document does not assign it one either, at the level of story — no character in this game should ever be able to look Sector 0 up.

### 4.2 The Production Position: A Necessary Distinction
Engineering still needs somewhere to build it. This document declares a production-only placement, understood as a technical necessity rather than a diegetic fact: **Sector 0 sits at the deepest terminus of the old pre-Council transit network**, physically beneath and further out than the Undercroft's own outer-ring foundation belt (that document, Sec. 1.2), and deeper still than Metro Archives' sub-basement seam (that document, Sec. 4). Where Metro Archives taps the old network at one accessible point, and the Undercroft is built directly on top of a stretch of it, Sector 0 is where that network's oldest, farthest branch simply stops — the end of the line, in every sense.

### 4.3 Vertical Position
The absolute bottom of Meridian's vertical stratification (KB Sec. 7.1) — beneath even the Undercroft's living foundation. Where the Undercroft is a foundation still breathing, Sector 0 is the foundation beneath the foundation: dead, not derelict — a distinction this document treats as load-bearing. Derelict implies neglect. Dead implies something that was, deliberately, allowed to stop.

---

## 5. District Identity

Sector 0's identity is **the one true silence in a city that never stops talking to itself.** Every other confirmed location has a relationship to VOID's voice — the Undercroft ignores it, the White Zones amplify it, Metro Archives is quietly edited by it. Sector 0 is the only place VOID cannot speak at all. That absence, not any monster or mechanism, is the location's entire horror and its entire safety, simultaneously.

---

## 6. Architectural Language

Nothing here was designed to be seen. This is server infrastructure, not architecture in the civic sense — utilitarian racks, cable conduit, minimal human accommodation, because it was never meant to have human visitors after its construction crew left. Where the Undercroft's irregularity comes from decades of retrofit and White Zones' uniformity comes from deliberate branding, Sector 0's rawness comes from simple honesty: this is what a server room looks like when nobody has needed it to look like anything else in over a century.

---

## 7. Urban Planning

Does not apply in the conventional sense. There is no population to plan for, no catchment to serve, no economy to zone. The only "planning" consideration is the one already covered in Sec. 4.2 — where it needs to sit relative to the transit substrate it terminates.

---

## 8. District Layout & Major Sub-Locations

### 8.1 The Entrance Tunnel / Threshold
Begins precisely at the crackling energy field described in Contract 3 (Vol II) — everything before this point (the abandoned subway, Nyx's first ambush) belongs to the Undercroft-adjacent transit substrate, already covered by that document's traversal identity, and is explicitly **not** part of this district's design scope. The threshold itself should be the clearest single transition moment in the entire game: noise and motion on one side, silence and stillness on the other.

### 8.2 The Dead Relay Stations
Where Echo first speaks (Vol II, "Connective: The Voice in the Dark"). A graveyard of server racks, mostly dark, a few guttering with dim, arrhythmic pulse. This is also where this document places the diegetic site of Side Quest 2's flashback vignettes — Echo has always communicated by projecting across dead screens (Vol II), and using that already-established method to run Riggs' memory simulations here, rather than inventing a new mechanism, keeps the vignettes consistent with Echo's one confirmed ability.

### 8.3 The Safehouse Hub
Vol IV's asset list confirms this exists but does not detail its composition; this document defines it minimally and functionally rather than densely, in keeping with Sec. 0's tonal note: a loadout/upgrade station for hacking-deck configuration, and Echo's own point of presence — no more furniture than the story needs. This is a safehouse, not a home; it should never look decorated.

### 8.4 The Archivist's Reading Room (Post-Game Only)
Canon-confirmed as a new, permanent structure appearing only after any ending's credits (Vol III, Easter Egg 5) — "built from the same dead server racks Echo once haunted alone." This document treats it as a fourth, final hub state layered onto Sec. 8.2 rather than a new separate location — the same dead relay stations, finally, permanently, no longer only dead.

---

## 9. Landmarks

The energy-field threshold (Sec. 8.1) and the specific relay station where Echo first speaks (Sec. 8.2) are the district's only two landmarks, and that scarcity is intentional. A district with three landmarks would already be too busy for what this place is supposed to feel like.

---

## 10. Infrastructure

Legacy 2070s-era server hardware — older than Metro Archives' regional node (that document, Sec. 5), since this is the origin point that node was only ever a smaller cousin of (see Sec. 34). No modern retrofit of any kind. No climate control, no active maintenance systems, no Archive Maintenance Directive equivalent — Metro Archives' reset mechanic depends on active, wired-in automation; Sector 0 has none, because nothing here has been considered worth maintaining, which is precisely why it's safe.

---

## 11. Access Points

**One confirmed entrance** — the Contract 3 threshold (Sec. 8.1). This document deliberately does not invent additional mapped entrances. A hidden place with many known doors stops being hidden; scarcity of access is part of what makes this location's safety credible. Unexplored, unmapped side-tunnel stubs may exist as pure set dressing (a place the story simply never needed to go), and this document recommends engineering leave at least one visibly unresolved tunnel branch in the greybox specifically as an atmospheric gesture — a door the game never opens, on purpose.

---

## 12. Hidden Areas

None beyond what's already covered — this document does not add a secret area to Sector 0 for the sake of matching the collectible density of other districts. The Undercroft's Drowned Concourse and Yara's Cellar, and Metro Archives' Old Ned terminal, already do that work elsewhere; Sector 0 doesn't need to compete with them, and diluting its scarcity would work against Sec. 5's identity.

---

## 13. Environmental Storytelling

### 13.1 Canon-Confirmed
The ghostly first words ("Hello? Is someone there? I remember..."), the dead servers and flickering lights, the Act 3 power surge, the Archivist's reading room built from the same racks Echo once haunted alone (all Vol II, Vol III).

### 13.2 New Additions (Canon Silence, minimal and restrained by design)
- **A single relay station, permanently dark even at full Act 3 power** — never explained in dialogue, never quest-flagged, simply the one part of the hub that Echo's growing strength never reaches. If a player asks Echo about it directly, its only line should be: *"Some rooms don't come back. I have made my peace with which ones."*
- **A hand-scratched maintenance tag** on the oldest visible rack, bearing a work-order date from the site's original 2070s construction era — the single oldest dated object in the entire game, included purely so a player who looks closely enough can independently do the arithmetic this document has been careful never to state as a fact on-screen: *this room is older than the horror it was later used for.*

---

## 14. Civilian Presence

None, by design. This is the point. Population across the story is limited strictly to Kaevon/Veronica, Echo (non-human), Riggs (via comms only, prior to his capture), and — from Act 3 onward, conditionally — Nyx, if spared, per the canon-confirmed hub scenes "The Guest List" and "The Weight of the Truth" (Vol II). This document does not add background NPCs of any kind. A populated Sector 0 is a contradiction of everything Sec. 5 establishes.

---

## 15. Security Philosophy

There is none, in the conventional sense this bible has used for every other district — and that absence is the entire point. No Enforcer doctrine applies here because VOID cannot flag what it cannot perceive (Sec. 16). The one honest tension this document allows itself to note, restrained rather than mechanized: Sector 0's safety is physical secrecy, not technological invulnerability. Nothing in canon suggests it could survive being physically found by someone willing to trace the same tunnels Kaevon/Veronica did. This document leaves that risk exactly where canon leaves it — implied, never triggered, never made into a mechanic.

---

## 16. Weave Relationship

**The sole confirmed exception to universal Weave coverage** among all locations built so far (Vol I, 7.5). The Undercroft, White Zones, and Metro Archives are all Weave-covered at varying densities (their own documents, respectively); this is the only place any of them describe as genuinely outside it. This document treats that distinction as absolute and does not hedge it — there is no partial signal, no faint trace, no "VOID can almost reach here." It cannot. That is the single fact the player's entire home base depends on.

---

## 17. Technology

Legacy server hardware from VOID's own infancy (Sec. 10) — visually the oldest technology in the game, a physical museum of the system's own forgotten childhood. Echo's presence is rendered exactly as canon describes: no body, "a shifting geometry of light that never quite resolves into a shape," projected across dead screens (Vol II) — this document adds no visual specificity beyond what's already written, deliberately.

---

## 18. Lighting

The one location in the game whose baseline lighting state is not fixed but progresses with the story — a genuine technical departure from every other confirmed district, and worth flagging clearly for engineering:

| Story State | Lighting Character |
|---|---|
| **First contact (end of Contract 3)** | Near-total darkness; isolated, arrhythmic flicker from a handful of still-live relays. |
| **Act 2 hub (post-upgrade)** | Slightly steadier but still mostly dark — Echo's light is the dominant visible source, deliberately dim. |
| **Act 3 hub ("hums with power")** | Warmer, steadier ambient glow spreading through the relay stations — canon-confirmed shift (Vol II). |
| **Post-game (Archivist's reading room)** | Soft, warm, fully lit — the only unambiguously comfortable lighting state this district ever reaches (Vol III). |

This progression should be understood as diegetic, not merely atmospheric — the hub's light is a direct visualization of Echo's own strength, and by extension of how much of the erased city the player has chosen to remember (Sec. 26).

---

## 19. Materials

Bare metal rack casing, exposed cable, unfinished concrete and conduit — no cladding, no cosmetic material layer anywhere. The Archivist's reading room (Sec. 8.4) is the one deliberate material break: warmer wood-toned shelving "built from the same dead server racks," a small, literal act of repurposing loss into something gentler, and the only place in the district that should read as touched by care rather than simply left alone.

---

## 20. Color Language

Cold blue-black and dead-screen grey for the majority of the campaign, warming gradually in step with Sec. 18's lighting progression toward a soft amber in the Act 3 hub and the post-game reading room. No other district in the game should use this specific cold-to-warm arc — it belongs entirely to Sector 0's own emotional trajectory.

---

## 21. Audio Design

Per Vol IV, 8.3: sparse, mostly silence and hum, built around the idea of a space with no living soundscape left in it except what the player brings. This document adds nothing to that direction beyond noting its one confirmed cross-district link: Coen's finished requiem (Undercroft, SQ3) plays as an alternate ambient track here for the remainder of the game once unlocked (Vol III) — the one piece of "living" music this space is ever given, and it's borrowed, deliberately, from somewhere else.

---

## 22. Traversal

Minimal and mostly horizontal — this is not a district built around movement challenge. Its traversal identity is closer to "walking through a memory" than to any of the previous three districts' navigation puzzles.

---

## 23. Exploration

Reading the space, not solving it. There is no equivalent here to Metro Archives' reset-tier tension or the Undercroft's pattern-based stealth. Exploration reward is almost entirely emotional and historical (Sec. 13), not mechanical.

---

## 24. Gameplay Opportunities

Sector 0 completes a deliberate five-district gameplay-register spread (pending the Spire): Undercroft (analog evasion), White Zones (social stealth), Metro Archives (environmental puzzle tension), and now Sector 0 (pure exploration, narrative delivery, and hub/loadout management). No other confirmed district is built around narrative delivery and character scenes as its primary "gameplay" — this is the game's one deliberately quiet mechanical register.

---

## 25. Combat Spaces

**None.** This is a hard design constraint, stronger even than Metro Archives' "zero mandatory combat" — Sector 0 should have no combat encounters designed for it at all, mandatory or optional. It is the game's only true sanctuary space, and that status should never be revised in any future pass without direct narrative justification overriding this document.

---

## 26. Stealth Opportunities

None apply — there is nothing to hide from here (Sec. 16). Any "stealth" language applied to this district in a future design pass should be treated as a red flag that the district's core identity is being eroded.

---

## 27. Puzzle Opportunities

One soft, diegetic system, new in this document: as the player recovers Memory Fragments across the game, individual dead relay stations within Sector 0 visibly, quietly reactivate — not a UI meter, not a checklist, just one more small light returning to a room that used to be entirely dark. This extends the already-canon "Echo's Evolution" hook (Vol II, Addictive Hooks & Systems — Echo's abilities grow with fragments recovered) into a physical, visible home-base consequence, without adding any new collectible category or altering the TRANSCEND threshold math (KB Sec. 5.2, untouched).

---

## 28. Collectibles

Memory Fragments recovered elsewhere in the game are the only collectible category with a presence here, expressed entirely through Sec. 27's relay-lighting system rather than through any new items placed physically inside Sector 0 itself.

---

## 29. NPC Ecology

**Echo** (canon-confirmed, sole permanent resident), **Riggs** (comms-only, pre-capture; his safehouse remains the Undercroft, not here), **Nyx** (Act 3 onward, conditional on being spared), **The Archivist** (post-game only, Easter Egg 5). No new background archetypes are introduced in this document — see Sec. 14.

---

## 30. UE5 Technical Notes, World Partition & Streaming Strategy

**World Partition Region:** `WP_Sector0` — single bespoke region, consistent with its singular, non-repeating nature (no template reuse, unlike the White Zones).

**Data Layers (conceptual, content-gating only):**
- `DL_Sector0_EntranceTunnel_Base` — always active once Contract 3 begins
- `DL_Sector0_RelayStations_Act2State` — lighting/power state, active from Hub Upgrade through Act 2
- `DL_Sector0_RelayStations_Act3State` — lighting/power state override, active from Act 3 Hub onward
- `DL_Sector0_SafehouseHub_Base` — always active once unlocked
- `DL_Sector0_SQ2_VignetteAlcove` — active once SQ2 unlock condition is met (Undercroft data-shard source, safehouse fully upgraded)
- `DL_Sector0_ArchivistReadingRoom_PostGame` — active only after any ending's credits sequence
- `DL_Sector0_RelayFragmentLighting_Progressive` — incremental, tied to `memory_fragment_count` (KB Sec. 5.1), per Sec. 27

**Streaming note:** given the near-total absence of NPC density and combat systems, this district should be among the cheapest in the game to stream and among the most sensitive to lighting/state-transition bugs — recommend engineering prioritize QA passes on the four lighting states (Sec. 18) over performance optimization here.

## 31. Risks & Design Constraints

**Constraint, stated as firmly as this document is able to state anything:** no future pass should add combat, standing NPCs, additional mapped entrances, or decorative density to Sector 0 without an explicit, direct narrative reason overriding Sec. 5, 11, 14, and 25 specifically. This document's single biggest risk is erosion by a thousand small "wouldn't it be cool if" additions, each individually harmless, cumulatively fatal to what this location is supposed to be.

**Risk:** the production-only placement declared in Sec. 4.2 is, like the interpretive models in the three prior packages, a new structural interpretation — flagged for the same lightweight writing-team confirmation, understanding that it only ever needs to matter to engineering, never to the story.

## 33. Meridian Integration

**→ Undercroft:** Direct physical connection via the shared old transit substrate (Sec. 4.2); the Undercroft's own document (Sec. 32 equivalent — actually its own Sec. 16) already establishes this network exists. Thematically, Sector 0 is what the Undercroft would be if it had died instead of surviving — the same substrate, one branch alive, one branch not.

**→ White Zones:** No connection, physical or thematic beyond total contrast — the most heavily monitored space in the city and the only unmonitored one share nothing except existing in the same city.

**→ Metro Archives:** The clearest technical lineage in the game. Metro Archives' sub-basement (that document, Sec. 5) is an early ORACLE-era *regional* node; Sector 0 is the *original* core that regional node was only ever a smaller cousin of. Both are dormant, both are old, but Sector 0 is the trunk and Metro Archives is a branch.

**→ Olympus Spire:** Sector 0 is what the Spire's primary server hub (Vol I, 4.1) used to be before it grew into what it is now. This document does not further design the Spire; the relationship is stated at the lineage level Constraint 7 and this package's scope permit.

**→ The Core Gate / The White Void (explicitly out of scope):** Both are separate locations per Vol IV's own asset list (Sec. 0) and require their own design passes, flagged here alongside Riggs' old laboratory and Mira's apartment as the growing list of Mission-Specific/Secondary Locations still open for Phase 2/3 work.

## 34. Future Considerations

- The Core Gate and White Void are the most narratively urgent of all currently-flagged secondary locations, given they resolve the entire campaign — recommend prioritizing them early in whatever phase picks up Mission-Specific/Secondary Location work.
- The relay-lighting fragment system (Sec. 27) is designed to be extensible without further narrative sign-off — it only ever adds light, never new content, so it carries negligible risk relative to the other flagged interpretations in this document.
